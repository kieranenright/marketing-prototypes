Naming the earning mechanic

Loyalty 2.0 · Decision summary for Brand
Decision Made
May 2026
formerly "Missions" → Goals
One noun, all channels, no qualifier. "Goals" replaces the internal working title "Missions" as the term for the things users do to earn points in Rewards.

Why "Goals" wins

  • Highest enticement of all candidates explored. Inherently motivating: users instinctively want to complete them.
  • Native vocabulary for the Efficient Inspired audience. Completionist instincts across gaming (17%), sports (20%), and financial groups (16%).
  • Passes TOV standards: carries the conviction, movement, and directionality our brand voice requires.
  • Translates cleanly across all markets (Ziel, objectif, doel, mål). No product language collisions.

Why no qualifier needed

  • EIs are life optimizers, not simplifiers. Quick goals feel satisfying, not overstated. They seek small wins throughout high output days.
  • Qualifiers like "easy goals" or "daily goals" dilute conviction and signal the system is apologising for itself.
  • "3 goals done. 1,200 points earned." is more satisfying than any softened alternative.
  • Investment framing over ease framing. Lead with what users put in and what they get back, not how little effort it takes.

How it sounds across channels

Push
"Free shipping. Three quick goals between you and it."
Email subject
"Your week has 3 goals. Your rewards have no ceiling."
Campaign
"You're already doing it. Now get rewarded for it."

What this means for Brand

Use "Goals" as the single noun in all materials. No dual terminology, no channel splitting. The copy around the noun creates the enticement: lead with the reward value, frame goals as an investment (put a little in, get a lot back), and write with conviction. Content Design and Marketing are aligned.
