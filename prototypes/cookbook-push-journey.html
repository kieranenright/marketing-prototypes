Efficient Inspired: Behind the Screen
The EI operates on a "penciled structure": they plan loosely, protect their autonomy to choose in the moment, and use tools to buy back time. 58% choose meals based on available preparation time. Food is both joyful passion and commodity to optimise. They don't want to be told what to do. They want options surfaced at the right moment so they can decide. Push notifications should feel like a smart system working on their behalf, not a brand nudging them.
Global Tone of Voice: The Battle for Midweek Dinnertime
Copy must be Fresh, Vivid, Sharp, and Zesty. Write in bursts, not paragraphs. Make food the hero. Say it with conviction. No filler.
Fresh
Energy and immediacy. Makes the everyday feel alive and new. "Tonight." "Your move." "Right now."
Vivid
Sensory cues and bold verbs. Words that sizzle and stick. "Calling." "Going viral." "The pan."
Sharp
Confident, concise, clutter-free. Fragments over sentences. No filler phrases or punctuation.
Zesty
Playful with a bite. Culturally tuned in, witty but warm. "Your rules." "Your call." "No problem."

1. Removed Meal Reminder 2 Variants
Trigger Event
User adds a meal to their weekly box, then removes it, then saves it to Cookbook via the in-app nudge. The recipe is now saved. This push reminds them to cook it.
EI Insight
Three intent signals: they added it (interest), removed it (chose flexibility over the kit), and saved it (confirmed they want it). The push isn't "don't forget" (patronising). It's a confident nudge that their system is working. The recipe is ready when they are. Autonomy preserved.
Suppression
  • Single shot per removal event. No cadence.
  • Another Cookbook push sent within past 5 days
  • Another push of any type within past 24 hours
  • Deep link: opens the saved recipe directly (not the save flow)

2. Meal Planner Gap Fill 3 Variants
Trigger Event
User has their meal planner active for the week. One or more days have no meal kit assigned. User has 1+ saved Cookbook recipes. Fires on the day of the gap, catching the "what's for dinner?" moment.
EI Insight
This is the "penciled structure" use case. They've deliberately left nights open because they want to decide in the moment. The push respects that by offering their own saved content as the answer, not prescribing it. Cookbook is their spontaneous prep toolkit for real-time optimisation.
Suppression

  • Skip if user already filled that day's slot with a Cookbook recipe

3. Skip Week 2 Variants
Trigger Event
User skips their HelloFresh box for the upcoming week. They still need to eat. Cookbook fills the gap without breaking their system.
EI Insight
They skipped the box but they're still life-optimizers running a household. They don't stop cooking, they stop receiving. Suppression

  • Only fires if user has 1+ saved recipes

6. Post-Pause / Cancel 2 Variants
Trigger Event

7. Trending Recipe 2 Variants
Trigger Event
A recipe hits a threshold of saves across the platform within a short window (e.g. 500+ saves in 24 hours). Social proof trigger that introduces Cookbook through cultural momentum.
EI Insight
EIs look for "culinary intel from accessible authorities" and are significantly more likely to try something new when they see social proof. 26% cite inspiration from friends' social posts. They actively seek cultural currency. Trending taps into their desire to be in the know without being a follower.
Suppression
  • Max 1 trending push per month
  • Only send to users who have opened the app in the last 14 days
  • Do not send if user has already saved the trending recipe
  • Threshold must be genuine (not artificially inflated by campaigns)

8. Missing Protein Suggestion 2 Variants
Trigger Event
User's box this week contains certain proteins (e.g. chicken, fish) but they have a saved Cookbook recipe that uses a protein NOT in this week's box (e.g. beef, lamb, prawns). We surface the gap as an opportunity: one ingredient to buy, one extra dinner sorted.
EI Insight
The EI is already planning their non-kit nights. They know what's in the box. HelloFresh sends exact portions, so there's no "extra" left over. But the EI shops for their off-nights anyway. Surfacing a saved recipe based on what's NOT covered this week gives them a specific, actionable shopping list item. One protein to grab, one dinner sorted. Cookbook becomes a smart companion that reads the gaps in their meal plan.
Suppression
  • Max 1 per delivery cycle
  • Only fire if protein match confidence is high (primary protein in the saved recipe, not a minor ingredient)
  • Do not send if user hasn't opened Cookbook in 30+ days
  • Do not stack with Box Delivery Day push (pick one or the other)

Global Rules 8 Rules
Rule Logic
Frequency Cap Max 1 Cookbook push per 5 days (across all use cases)
AM Window 07:30 to 09:00 local. For planning/discovery angles. Targets the EI morning social scroll (per Behind the Screen: morning anchor = social media browsing, planning mode active).
PM Window 17:00 to 19:00 local. For "cook tonight" and action-oriented pushes. Catches the EI's dinner decision moment when they finalise their spontaneous plans (real-time optimisation zone).
24h Suppression Do not send if user received any other push (from any team) in the last 24 hours
Event Priority If multiple events fire in the same window, prioritise: Removed Meal > Meal Planner Gap > Missing Protein > High-Rated Kit > Skip Week > Box Delivery > Post-Pause > Trending. Most personalised and most time-sensitive wins.
Opt-out Respect Immediate suppression on push opt-out. No fallback to other channels from this strategy.
Personalisation Fallback If recipe name exceeds ~20 characters in title, move recipe name to body and use generic title variant. Never truncate mid-word.
Deep Link Standard All pushes deep link to the specific recipe in Cookbook when personalised, or to the Cookbook home when generic. Never link to a save flow if the recipe is already saved.

Open Questions / Next Steps
  1. 1Data availability: Can we access meal planner gap days, box meal count, removed meal events, and recipe ratings from the push platform in real time?
  2. 2Personalisation depth: Which variables are available? Recipe name, cook time, saved count, ingredient list, box meal count, day of week?
  3. 3Trending threshold: What save count qualifies as "trending"? Need to define threshold per market (smaller markets need lower bars).
  4. 4Ingredient matching logic: How confident can we be in matching saved recipe ingredients to box contents? Need data team to scope.
  5. 5Cross-team coordination: These pushes interact with delivery notifications, retention messaging, and marketing pushes. Need alignment on 24h suppression windows across teams.
  6. 6Localisation: US only or multi-market? Global TOV applies everywhere but character counts may shift with translation.
